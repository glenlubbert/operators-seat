# Operator's Seat: Start Here

*An AI chief of staff for you and your business, built for privacy and portability.*

---

For too long, I had to keep re-explaining myself and constantly uploading files in each AI conversation or repeat myself when switching between AI apps. Sometimes it made it into memory, or I built a skill around it. Then I'd switch to a different AI and start from scratch. And all that repeating burned through my tokens. More than once I hit my limit in the middle of a project and had to wait hours for it to reset before I could keep going. So I began recording it all in plain text files, in the same set of folders I've used since 2017 to organize my digital life, for myself and for my businesses.

That set of folders, now easier to manage and with much of the work automated by agents, runs my company: marketing, finance, operations, and product development. Every Friday it writes the marketing report my co-founder reads before our call and the week's financial snapshot. It keeps the product build specs, syncs the CRM, knows our pricing and our voice rules, which leads are warm and which are dead, and it files what we decide the moment we decide it. I don't re-explain anything. And it isn't tied to one AI: Claude, ChatGPT, or an open-source one on my laptop with the wifi off.

As I've talked about this system, people have asked me to share it, and I finally got around to packaging it as Operator's Seat. Whether you run a business or the business of your life, it's your own chief of staff, in files you own, so you stay independent of any one AI.

I broke it down into seven steps. You can do one a day or back-to-back.

## What you'll walk away with

By step 7 you'll have a set of folders any AI can read that knows who you are, what you've decided, and what you've said no to. It will file new decisions as you make them. And you'll have the one habit that keeps it alive: fifteen minutes on Friday.

Step 1 takes about half an hour; the rest are under fifteen minutes. Do them in order since the later steps only make sense once the earlier ones are real.

## What you need

- **A desktop or laptop** (Mac or Windows). The phone apps can't open folders on your computer.
- **An AI app that can work in a folder on your computer.** As of October 2026 that means a paid plan: the Claude desktop app on Pro or higher, or ChatGPT's desktop app on Plus or higher. The free plans don't let the AI open your folders. Plans change, so if you're not sure, check your app's plan page.
- **No paid plan?** An open-source AI on your own computer is free; `Starter/Level 2 - Operator Layer/Run it on your own machine.md` explains it. It's more technical to set up and hasn't been fully tested with these steps yet.

## What's in this folder

- **START HERE.md**: you're reading it.
- **Starter/**: the folder you copy. It becomes your operating system.
- **Cheat Sheet - Operator's Seat.pdf**: the seven steps on one page. Print it.

## Step 1: Copy the folder and let it interview you

Unzip what I sent you. Copy `Starter/` to wherever you keep your work (Dropbox, iCloud, a plain folder on your desk). Rename it: your name, your company, whatever you'll recognize. Mine is "Glen OS." Others: "Maria's Seat," "Bright Path HQ," "Chief of Staff."

Now attach it to your AI's desktop app as a project, so every conversation can see it:

- **Claude:** in the left sidebar, click **Projects**, then **+**, then **Use an existing folder**. Pick your folder and click **Create**.
- **ChatGPT:** switch to **Work**, create a project, then **Edit project**, **Add folder**, pick your folder, and choose **Make primary**.

Then type the same line in either one:

> **Read AGENTS.md, then interview me.**

That line is all the setup there is. The folder tells the AI how to work, and if anything in your app needs changing, the AI will tell you.

It asks a dozen questions, one at a time: what you do, your best hopes for the week, what's already working, what you've decided, what you're still figuring out, who matters, how you like to be talked to, then what you own, what you decide, and what you've said no to. Last, it asks for a list of the projects you're working on right now (just the names) and suggests a starting set of areas, the ongoing parts of your work or life, depending on whether this is for a business, your life, or both. You edit the list; it makes a folder for each. You answer like you'd answer a new assistant on their first morning. Don't try to be complete. A sentence is enough, and "skip" is a fine answer. It fills in the gaps as you work with it; that's what the rest of the steps are for. At the end it shows you what it wrote, you fix anything that's off, and it saves the file. You never open the file yourself.

**Done when:** it reads your answers back and you'd sign them.

## Step 2: Catch it being wrong

Open your project from the sidebar, start a new conversation, and ask something you'd ask an assistant who'd been there a while:

> What should I be careful about this week, given what you know about me?

Somewhere in the answer it'll get something wrong, or miss something you'd assumed was obvious. Tell it. It should offer to update the file; if it doesn't, say "Save that to Glen OS" (or whatever you named your folder). Name the folder, because Claude and ChatGPT both have their own built-in memory, and a plain "save that" can land there instead, where the next AI can't see it. And it isn't just for corrections: in the middle of any conversation, about anything, you can say "Save that to Glen OS" and it goes in your folders. That's the whole habit, and it's the difference between an AI with a memory and a chat that forgets: corrections go in the file, not the conversation.

**Done when:** you've corrected it once and it saved the correction.

## Step 3: Tell it five true things

Say:

> Here are some things that are true about my work. File them in Glen OS.

(Use your folder's name, same as step 2.)

Then talk. What the company does. What you charge. The people who matter and why. A decision you made and the reason. Five to ten things. It writes each one into its memory with today's date and tells you what it filed.

Or, if you'd rather hand it what you already have: drag a few files into the `0. Inbox` folder (the documents you'd give a new assistant on day one) and say "Sort my inbox." It tells you what each one is, suggests where it belongs, and pulls out what's worth remembering. Nothing moves until you say yes. (Some AI apps can write files but not move them. If yours can't, it gives you a short list of what to drag where, and checks once you've done it.) Start with a handful, not your whole hard drive. You can use the inbox any day after this, too.

Either way, in a new conversation, ask a question only those facts can answer. It should cite them back to you.

**Done when:** the AI answers from what you told it instead of guessing.

## Step 4: Fill in one project

Step 1 made a folder for each project you named, inside `1. Projects`. That's the first of your four folders: Projects is anything with a finish line. (These are projects inside your folders, not projects in the Claude or ChatGPT app; you only make that once, in step 1.)

Pick the one that matters most this week and say:

> Let's fill in [project name].

It asks what done looks like, the deadline, and who's involved, and writes it into that project's notes. From now on, any conversation that touches that project reads its notes first. Have a new one later? Say "I want to add a project to Glen OS."

Already have files for it? Drag them into the project's folder and say "Read what's in there and tell me what matters."

Try it: new conversation, ask what's due on it.

**Done when:** it knows the deadline.

## Step 5: Give it one rule

Think of something you've corrected it on twice, or anything you already know you always want. A word you never use. A format you always want. A suggestion you keep rejecting. Say:

> From now on, [the rule]. Make that a rule in Glen OS.

It files it in your rules file, where every AI you use will see it, and tells you. To be sure it landed in your folders and not in the app's own memory, ask: "Show me the line you saved and the file it's in." It should name a file in your folder, like `knowledge/rules.md`. If it says it saved to memory, say "Put it in Glen OS instead." This is where the folder starts getting smarter instead of just bigger.

**Done when:** it's shown you the line in a file in your folder, and the next conversation follows the rule without a reminder.

## Step 6: The Friday audit, once, together

Whatever day it is, do this once now. After today, it's Fridays. Say:

> Run the Friday audit.

It asks the seat's questions (best hopes, what's working, where you are on a 0 to 10 scale, smallest next step), tells you what's gone stale, suggests a rule it thinks has earned its place, and tells you what it keeps having to ask about. Approve what's right; it saves. Then the reminder: say "Put fifteen minutes on my calendar every Friday for this." If your AI has a calendar connected, it creates the event and tells you; if not, it says so and you make the event yourself. Either way it exists before you close the conversation.

This is the habit. Everything else is files.

**Done when:** the Friday event is on your calendar and your seat has today's date under Now (ask it to read Now back to you).

## Step 7: Set one thing to run on its own

Say:

> Run the first feed.

The first feed is the weekly stale-knowledge check: it reads your knowledge files and comes back with what hasn't been reviewed in a while, so your Friday audit starts with a list instead of a blank page. Read what it found. If the list is useful, say:

> Schedule that for Friday mornings.

In Claude, the AI writes out the finished prompt for you; click **Scheduled** in the sidebar, create a new task, and paste it in. After the first scheduled run, check that it could open your folder; if it couldn't, keep the Claude app open on your computer when it's due.

If your AI doesn't do scheduled tasks yet, run it by hand on Fridays for a month. The habit matters more than the automation.

**Done when:** it's run once by hand, you've read what it found, and the schedule exists (or you've decided to run it by hand for now).

## That's it

Seven steps. One habit. You never opened a file, and you made every decision; the AI only did the typing. The AI files decisions as you go, and you do Fridays. Skip Fridays and the whole thing rots in about six weeks; I've watched it happen to mine.

The first version will be wrong. The third will be useful. The tenth will quietly change how you work.

**When you want more:** `Starter/Level 2 - Operator Layer/README.md` is where this grows into something that runs a business. Don't open it until you've done a few Fridays.

**Already have a vault?** `Starter/README.md` has the steps for nesting this inside Obsidian or an existing PARA setup.

**Switching AIs.** This is the whole point. Attach the same folder in the other app and start with "Read AGENTS.md first." It reads what the last AI saved and picks up where you left off; no second interview. Everything lives in your folders, not in any app's memory, so nothing is lost when you switch. You can also use two at once: after one AI drafts something that matters, open the folder in the other and say "Double-check [the draft or file]." A second AI catches things the first one missed. Use a desktop or laptop; the phone apps can't open folders on your computer.

**AI on your own laptop?** `Starter/Level 2 - Operator Layer/Run it on your own machine.md` covers installing an open-source AI on your laptop. Set up fully locally, as that page describes, nothing leaves your machine. Same folder, same line to start.
